## Definitions and Key Statistical Concepts

*   **Joint Event**: An event that depends on classes from two different categorical variables (0:58).
*   **Joint Probability**: The probability of the intersection of two events occurring simultaneously (2:03).
*   **Marginal Probability (Simple Probability)**: The raw probability of an event occurring, calculated by summing the relevant joint probabilities; it ignores the other variable (2:55).
*   **Union**: The probability of event A or event B occurring (5:47).
*   **Conditional Probability**: The probability of an event occurring given that another event has already occurred (7:07).
*   **Independent Variables**: Two variables are independent if the probability of one is not affected by the condition of the other; formally, the probability of A given B equals the probability of A (10:40).

    *   Here, $P(A cap B)$ is subtracted because it is counted twice when summing the two marginal probabilities (6:12).
*   **Conditional Probability**: $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A cap B)}{P(B)}$
    *   Where $P(A|B)$ is the probability of A given B, $P(A cap B)$ is the joint probability, and $P(B)$ is the probability of the condition (7:23).
*   **Test for Independence (Method 1)**: $P(A|B) = P(A)$

## Conditional Expectation and the Total Expectation Theorem (0:00-6:10)

### Definitions

*   **Conditional Expectation:** Given a random variable $Y$ takes on a specific value $y$, the conditional expectation of $X$ given $Y=y$ is the usual expectation calculated using the relevant conditional probability mass function (PMF): $E[X | Y=y] = sum_x x cdot P(X=x | Y=y)$. (0:55)
*   **Scenario:** A partition of the sample space into disjoint events (e.g., the event that $Y$ takes on a specific value $y_i$). (2:09)

### Formulas, Variables & Derivations

*   **Total Probability Theorem:** Used to find the probability of $X=x$ by partitioning the sample space into scenarios based on $Y=y_i$:
    $$P(X=x) = sum_i P(Y=y_i) cdot P(X=x | Y=y_i)$$
    (3:11)
*   **Total Expectation Theorem:** Used to find the expected value of $X$ by weighting conditional expectations across all disjoint scenarios of $Y$:
    $$E[X] = sum_i P(Y=y_i) cdot E[X | Y=y_i]$$
    (4:09)

### Mathematical Nuances

*   **Finite vs. Infinite Sets:** While the theorems are derived using a finite partition of the sample space, they remain valid for discrete but infinite sets (e.g., $Y$ ranging over all integers), provided $X$ has a well-defined expectation. (4:42-5:34)

### Key Concepts

*   **Divide and Conquer:** The Total Expectation Theorem serves as a powerful tool for breaking down complicated models into simpler, conditional components. (6:02)

## Conditional Probability Mass Functions (PMFs)

### Definitions

*   **Conditional PMF of X given Y:** For a random variable $X$ conditioned on the event that another random variable $Y$ takes a specific value $y$, the conditional PMF is defined as the ratio of the joint PMF to the marginal PMF of the conditioning event (1:28).

### Formulas

*   **Definition of Conditional PMF:**
    $$p_{X|Y}(x|y) = \frac{p_{X,Y}(x,y)}{p_Y(y)}$$
    *   This is defined only for values of $y$ where $p_Y(y) > 0$ (1:45).
*   **Multiplication Rule (Two Variables):**
    $$p_{X,Y}(x,y) = p_Y(y) cdot p_{X|Y}(x|y)$$
    *   This allows for specifying a probability model by defining the marginal distribution of $Y$ and the conditional PMF of $X$ given $Y$ (7:05).
*   **General Multiplication Rule (Three Variables):**
    $$p_{X,Y,Z}(x,y,z) = p_X(x) cdot p_{Y|X}(y|x) cdot p_{Z|X,Y}(z|x,y)$$
    *   Represents the probability of the simultaneous occurrence of three events as a product of individual and conditional probabilities (10:23).

### Example: Computing Conditional PMF

*   **Scenario:** Given a joint PMF table, calculate the conditional PMF of $X$ given $Y=2$ (3:21).
*   **Steps:**
    1.  **Identify the conditioning event:** Find the row in the joint PMF table corresponding to $Y=2$ (3:28).
    2.  **Calculate the marginal PMF:** Sum the entries in the row to find $p_Y(2)$. In the example, the sum is $5/20$ (3:45).
    3.  **Normalize:** Divide each entry in the row by $p_Y(2)$ so that the resulting values sum to $1$. For instance, the probability of $X=2$ given $Y=2$ becomes $(1/20) / (5/20) = 1/5$ (4:24).
    4.  **Visualization:** The resulting conditional PMF preserves the proportions of the original joint PMF slice but is scaled (renormalized) to total $1$ (5:59).

## Definitions and Formulas for Independence of Random Variables (0:00-5:08)

### Definitions

*   **Independence of Random Variables:** Two random variables $X$ and $Y$ are independent if their joint probability mass function (PMF) factors into the product of their individual marginal PMFs for all possible values of $x$ and $y$.
*   **Generalization to Events:** Independence is defined by the condition that the joint probability of events related to $X$ and $Y$ occurring is the product of their individual probabilities: $P(X in A, Y in B) = P(X in A)P(Y in B)$ for any sets $A$ and $B$.

### Formulas

*   **Condition for Independence (Discrete Case):**
    $$p_{X,Y}(x,y) = p_X(x)p_Y(y) quad \text{for all } x, y$$
*   **Alternative Expression via Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF):**
    $$F_{X,Y}(x,y) = F_X(x)F_Y(y) quad \text{for all } x, y$$
*   **Expectation of a Product of Independent Random Variables:**
    $$E[XY] = E[X]E[Y]$$
*   **Variance of a Sum of Independent Random Variables:**
    $$\text{var}(X+Y) = \text{var}(X) + \text{var}(Y)$$

## Joint PMF Definitions and Formulas (0:00-10:11)

### Definitions

*   **Joint PMF:** The probability that random variables $X$ and $Y$ take on specific values $x$ and $y$ simultaneously, denoted as $p_{X,Y}(x, y) = P(X = x, Y = y)$. (1:24-1:56)
*   **Marginal PMF:** The individual probability distribution of a single random variable derived from the joint PMF by summing over all possible values of the other random variable(s). (3:02-4:40)

### Key Formulas & Mathematical Rules

*   **Joint PMF Property (Normalization):** The sum of the joint probabilities over all possible pairs $(x, y)$ must equal 1.
    $$sum_{x} sum_{y} p_{X,Y}(x, y) = 1$$ (2:42-3:01)
*   **Finding Marginal PMF (for two variables):**
    *   To find $p_X(x)$: $p_X(x) = sum_{y} p_{X,Y}(x, y)$ (3:36-4:25)
    *   To find $p_Y(y)$: $p_Y(y) = sum_{x} p_{X,Y}(x, y)$ (4:37-5:17)
*   **Joint PMF for Multiple Variables (Three-Variable Case):** Defined as $p_{X,Y,Z}(x, y, z) = P(X=x, Y=y, Z=z)$, where the sum over all triples equals 1. (6:01-6:59)
*   **Marginalization for Multiple Variables:**
    *   For one variable: $p_X(x) = sum_{y} sum_{z} p_{X,Y,Z}(x, y, z)$ (7:00-7:29)
    *   For two variables: $p_{X,Y}(x, y) = sum_{z} p_{X,Y,Z}(x, y, z)$ (7:30-8:02)
*   **Expected Value Rule for Functions of Two Random Variables:** If $Z = g(X, Y)$, the expected value is the sum over all pairs weighted by their joint probabilities:
    $$E[g(X, Y)] = sum_{x} sum_{y} g(x, y) p_{X,Y}(x, y)$$ (9:27-10:11)

## Definitions and Core Concepts (0:00-7:09)

*   **Independence of Random Variables**: Two random variables $X$ and $Y$ are independent if the expected value of their product equals the product of their individual expected values: $E[XY] = E[X]E[Y]$ (2:07-2:39).
*   **Indicator Variable**: A random variable $X_i$ that takes the value $1$ if a specific trial results in a success, and $0$ otherwise (5:26-5:37).
*   **Binomial Random Variable**: A random variable representing the total number of successes in $n$ independent trials (5:11-5:16).

## Variance Formulas

*   **Variance of a Sum (General)**: In general, the variance of the sum of two random variables is not necessarily equal to the sum of their variances (0:22-0:30).
*   **Variance of a Sum (Independent)**: If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, the variance of their sum is the sum of their variances: $Var(X + Y) = Var(X) + Var(Y)$ (0:33-0:43).
*   **Variance of a Constant Multiple**: $Var(cX) = c^2 Var(X)$ (3:18-3:24).
*   **Variance of a Bernoulli Variable**: For an indicator variable $X_i$ with parameter $p$, the variance is $Var(X_i) = p(1 - p)$ (6:45-6:55).
*   **Variance of a Binomial Variable**: Given $X$ is a sum of $n$ independent indicator variables $X_i$, $Var(X) = n 	imes Var(X_1) = np(1 - p)$ (6:12-7:02).

## Examples Used

*   **Identical Variables**: If $X = Y$, then $Var(X + Y) = Var(2X) = 4Var(X)$ (2:51-3:24).
*   **Negative Correlation**: If $X = -Y$, then $X + Y = 0$, so $Var(X + Y) = 0$ (3:28-3:51).
*   **Scaling Independence**: If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, $Var(X - 3Y) = Var(X) + Var(-3Y) = Var(X) + 9Var(Y)$ (4:21-4:58).
